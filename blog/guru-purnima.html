Guru Purnima:
Honouring the Light That Guides Our Lives

The full moon of Ashadha is not merely a festival. It is a day to bow in gratitude to every teacher, sage and grace that has moved us from darkness towards wisdom.

Jyogi July 2026 8 min read
GURU PURNIMA VYASA PURNIMA GURU MANTRA BRIHASPATI SPIRITUAL PRACTICE
✦ गुरु पूर्णिमा — Ashadha Purnima, the full moon of the Guru ✦

There is a kind of knowledge that no book can give and no algorithm can replicate. It arrives through a relationship — between a seeker and a guide who has already walked the path.

Guru Purnima is the day Sanatan Dharma pauses to acknowledge this truth. Observed on the full-moon night of Ashadha, it is a day of gratitude, humility and spiritual renewal. And every year, as I sit in reflection on this sacred Purnima, one name rises in my heart before all others.

A Personal Note: Gratitude to Shree Kripaluji Maharaj

✦ GURU PRANAAM · WITH FULL SURRENDER ✦

श्री कृपालु जी महाराज — Jagadguru Shree Kripaluji Maharaj

Before I speak of Guru Purnima in any broad or traditional sense, I must first bow.

Shree Kripaluji Maharaj — the fifth original Jagadguru of Sanatan Dharma — was not simply a teacher. He was a living demonstration of what the Guru tradition truly means: pure, unconditional, inexhaustible love flowing towards the seeker, asking for nothing in return.

His message was always the same: "God and Guru are identical. The Guru is the one who reveals this truth — not in theory, but within your own heart."

His compositions in Braj Bhasha — the Prem Ras Madira, the Bhakti Shatak, thousands of bhajans describing the pain of separation and the sweetness of divine love — have a quality I have found nowhere else. They do not merely describe devotion. They induce it.

On this Guru Purnima, I offer my deepest pranaam at his lotus feet. Whatever capacity for love, whatever fragment of wisdom finds its way into my work — let it be credited to his grace alone.

What Is the Meaning of Guru Purnima?

The word Guru is not simply a synonym for teacher. The Sanskrit etymology is precise:

✦ GURU — THE ETYMOLOGY ✦
गुकारस्त्वन्धकारस्तु रुकारस्तन्निवर्तकः।
अन्धकारनिरोधित्वात् गुरुरित्यभिधीयते॥
The syllable "Gu" represents darkness or ignorance. The syllable "Ru" represents its removal.
A Guru is the one who dispels the darkness of ignorance.

Purnima means the full-moon night — the sky at its brightest, most complete. The Guru and the full moon share the same quality: they reflect the light of the Supreme so perfectly that even the darkest night becomes navigable.

Guru Purnima is observed on the full-moon day of Ashadha (June–July). Ashadha marks the beginning of the monsoon — a period of inwardness, retreat from the external world. It is the ideal season to turn towards the inner Guru.

Why Is It Also Called Vyasa Purnima?

The day is equally known as Vyasa Purnima in honour of Maharishi Ved Vyasa — one of the most significant sages in the Vedic tradition.

Vyasa organised the four Vedas into their present form, composed the eighteen major Puranas, and wrote the Mahabharata — within which the Bhagavad Gita is embedded. He made the vast ocean of Vedic knowledge accessible to humanity across time. He is considered the first and foremost Guru of the Vedic lineage — the Adi-Guru of the written word.

✦ Maharishi Ved Vyasa — the Adi-Guru of the Vedic written tradition ✦
✦ NOTE ON LINEAGE ✦ The Guru-Shishya Parampara — the sacred lineage through which spiritual knowledge passes from teacher to student — traces its origin to Vyasa and beyond. Kripaluji Maharaj was recognised by the four existing Jagadgurus of his era as the fifth, the first in 150 years. This was not a ceremonial title. It was an acknowledgement that the living lineage had chosen its continuation.

How to Observe Guru Purnima

Elaborate rituals are not compulsory. The most valuable offering is sincere gratitude expressed through a genuinely changed life.

PRACTICE ONE
Remember Your Gurus
Begin the day by mentally bowing to your spiritual master, teachers, parents and every person who gave you something real. A few minutes of silence before any other activity.
PRACTICE TWO
Perform Guru Puja
Light a diya. Place the Guru's photograph or Guru Paduka. Offer flowers and fruits. Chant the Guru mantra. Sit in silent meditation. The outer puja is the invitation; the inner stillness is the meeting.
PRACTICE THREE
Study Sacred Teachings
Read one chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, Guru Gita or the teachings of your own Guru. Find one instruction you can actually practise in the coming month.
PRACTICE FOUR
Offer Seva
Selfless service — to a spiritual institution, to your teacher, your parents, or someone genuinely in need. Seva done without expectation is the fastest purifier of the mind.
PRACTICE FIVE
Begin or Renew a Discipline
Guru Purnima is auspicious for beginning mantra chanting, meditation, pranayama or daily scriptural reading. Choose one practice that can be continued consistently.
PRACTICE SIX
Sit Under the Full Moon
Spend some time in the moonlight on this night. Offer your thoughts, confusion and gratitude to the sky. The full moon on Ashadha Purnima carries its own quiet grace.

What Guru Purnima Is Really Asking

Have I become a better student of life?
Am I willing to be corrected?
Do my actions honour what I have been taught?

Kripaluji Maharaj often said that the greatest sign of the Guru's grace is not visions or siddhis. The greatest sign is this: that you are becoming more loving, more humble, more genuinely kind — slowly, without drama, over time. If that is happening — however gradually — the Guru's work is being done.

O Divine Guru,
remove ignorance from my mind,
pride from my heart
and confusion from my path.

Bless me with the wisdom to recognise what is true,
the courage to practise it
and the humility to remain a student.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Guru Purnima?
Guru Purnima is a sacred full-moon day observed on the Purnima of the Hindu month of Ashadha, dedicated to honouring spiritual masters, teachers, parents and mentors who guide us from ignorance towards wisdom.
Why is it also called Vyasa Purnima?
The day honours Maharishi Ved Vyasa, who organised the four Vedas and composed the Mahabharata and Puranas, making the entire ocean of Vedic knowledge accessible to humanity. He is considered the first Guru of the written Vedic tradition.
Can I observe Guru Purnima without a living spiritual Guru?
Yes. You may honour your teachers, parents, mentors, a chosen deity or the divine Guru principle through prayer, meditation, mantra chanting and gratitude. The Guru principle — the force that removes ignorance — is always accessible.
Which mantra is recited on Guru Purnima?
The mantra beginning with "Gurur Brahma Gurur Vishnu" is the most widely recited. The simpler "Om Shri Gurubhyo Namah" may be chanted throughout the day with full attention and genuine feeling.
Is Guru Purnima connected with Jupiter in Vedic astrology?
Jupiter is called Guru or Brihaspati in Vedic astrology and represents wisdom, teachers and dharma. The observance is primarily spiritual — rooted in the Guru-Shishya Parampara — but both share the same symbolic root of knowledge and guidance.
Who was Jagadguru Kripaluji Maharaj?
Jagadguru Shree Kripaluji Maharaj (1922–2013) was the fifth original Jagadguru of Sanatan Dharma, recognised by all four existing Jagadgurus of his era after hearing him speak for seven consecutive days across Vedas, Upanishads, Brahma Sutras and Bhagavatam without repetition. He taught the path of divine love (bhakti) as the supreme means to God-realisation, and composed thousands of devotional works in Braj Bhasha, including the Prem Ras Madira.
